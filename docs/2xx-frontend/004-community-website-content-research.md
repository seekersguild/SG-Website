# Community Website Content Research

Research date: 22 November 2026, Asia/Manila.

## Rationale

Seekers Guild's public website should help a student understand the community, attend a first gathering, understand membership, and find ways to contribute. It should also help a school, mentor, company or venue propose a collaboration. This recommendation follows a review of the current repository, the deployed site inspected earlier in this session, and the first-party websites listed below.

The comparison covers seven community organisations: Socratica, Hack Club, Recurse Center, DEVCON Philippines, Design Buddies, Google's developer communities, and Superteam Philippines' public opportunity profile. They differ substantially in audience, resources, maturity and admission model. Their published website patterns are useful evidence of approaches, not proof that a particular feature increases conversion. No conversion data or private member research was available. Recommendations below are the researcher's synthesis for Seekers Guild.

A background research worker was attempted but had no callable tools. The primary agent performed all source verification and synthesis directly.

## Second verification and revised priorities

Rechecked on 22 November 2026 by fetching the deployed homepage again (HTTP 200), comparing its visible text with `app/page.tsx`, `app/content/site.ts` and the event components, and revisiting all seven comparison organisations' cited entry pages. This pass rechecked content and evidence; it did not repeat the earlier rendered-layout inspection.

The original recommendation list included both improvements to existing information and genuinely absent content. Use this narrower interpretation:

| Finding | Verified state | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eligibility and nontechnical participation | The live Who we are section and FAQ already name technology, business, creative and events interests, and college/senior-high students. | Improve consistency and prominence; do not add a duplicate eligibility section. |
| Costs and first-visit basics | Free membership, public attendance, no pitch/agenda, food/drink expenses and RSVP on Luma are already explained. | Preserve these facts and make them easier to find. Add only unanswered arrival/preparation/welcome details, using the actual host's practices. |
| Contact | The joining section already includes Message us on Facebook, alongside social links elsewhere. | A contact route exists. A dedicated email or additional form is optional; clarify enquiry ownership if needed. |
| Programmes | All four programmes already have descriptions, cadence and access labels. | Add current status and a usable next action where confirmed. Do not rebuild the programme catalogue or invent dates/availability. |
| Joining | The two-endorsement requirement and short review are stated. | The unresolved mechanics and membership expectations are genuine information gaps. |
| Community evidence | The page displays symbols, atmosphere, event posters and a past-event list; no substantive member project stories, gathering-photo stories or attributed testimonials were found. | Begin with actual recaps and photographs. A project showcase is appropriate when publishable member work exists. |
| Province/remote participation | The mission and outreach programme mention provinces, but no practical route for someone unable to attend Manila gatherings was found. | Ask the guild to establish or clarify the supported route. Website copy cannot create an operational programme. |
| Volunteering and partnerships | No dedicated enquiry route or role descriptions were found. | Add concise routes when the team wants and can handle those enquiries. A full recruitment or sponsorship system is optional. |
| Updates | Luma and social channels already exist. | Highlight a dependable existing channel first. A newsletter or separate signup is optional. |

**First priority:** publish real community evidence, clarify endorsement/review mechanics, and improve the first-visit explanation and placement of current information. Include consistent audience wording and event-specific logistics in that pass.

**Guild decisions first:** regional/remote access, active volunteer roles, partnership offers, programme status, and any new membership commitments. Record the actual answers before building extra workflows.

**Optional later:** dedicated newsletters, directories, opportunity boards, mentorship systems, job boards and member portals. None is established as essential by the available evidence.

Comparison sources have different weight. Socratica is the closest reference for a welcoming recurring gathering; DEVCON is a relevant Philippine example of local participation and collaboration; Recurse is useful for explaining a selective joining process, with a substantially different programme model. Hack Club and Design Buddies support particular content patterns. Google's broad community landing page and Superteam's Web3 opportunity profile are supplementary references and do not justify adding platform features to this guild's site. This weighting is the researcher's judgment, not a measured ranking.

## Four visitor needs

| Visitor | Questions the site should answer | Useful next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-time student or young builder | Is this for me? Are beginners/nontechnical people welcome? Can I come alone? What happens? What will it cost? | View and RSVP to the next public gathering. |
| Prospective member | What changes with membership? What is expected? How do endorsements and review work? | Follow the actual introduction or application process. |
| Existing member or contributor | What is happening? Where can I help? What are others making? | Find a current event, volunteer opening or project contribution contact. |
| School, mentor, company or venue | What can we do together? Who participates? What support is useful? Who is responsible? | Send a scoped partnership enquiry. |

Do not give all four routes equal visual emphasis on the homepage. The recommended primary action is attendance at the next suitable public gathering. Membership and contributor routes should be easy to find. Partnerships can be a quieter link.

## Practical material already available

The guild's [current public Open Table listing](https://luma.com/hf20kfv8) already explains that the gathering is public, core members will be present, and attendees purchase their own food and drinks. It gives event-specific hours and states that registration reveals the exact venue. Its description explicitly welcomes returning and new attendees.

Bring those practical facts into the website's first-visit explanation, with event-specific details remaining authoritative in Luma. Do not publish a registration-gated venue address or republish attendee names as a website member directory. The site currently describes typical Open Table timing differently from the event listing; distinguish a typical schedule from the actual next event.

The existing programme, joining and FAQ copy in `app/content/site.ts` is an important starting point. It describes Open Table, a builder retreat, outreach and recognition. The researcher did not find sufficient first-party public evidence to independently verify the current operational status, funding, available seats or exact admission procedure of every programme. Confirm those details with their owners before expanding the claims.

## Proposed homepage sequence

1. Guild name and motto, followed immediately by one concrete explanation of who participates and what they do.
2. Next public gathering with its direct RSVP and essential logistics.
3. Community in action: real people, a short event story and member work.
4. What participants can do: active programmes with clear entry routes.
5. Start Here: first visit, membership distinction, and getting introduced.
6. Ways to contribute or collaborate, including regional enquiries if supported.
7. Short guild story and values, with the full charter available for interested readers.
8. FAQ, contact and a dependable updates route.

Suggested navigation: Gatherings, What We Do, Community, About, Start Here. Keep one prominent attendance action. Footer links can carry partnership enquiries, contact, conduct and privacy information. These labels are proposals to validate against the final content.

Begin with sections on the existing site. A dedicated Start Here page becomes useful if first-visit and membership information becomes too long for the homepage. Stories can get individual URLs once there are substantive entries. A partnership page is justified when the team has concrete formats and a monitored contact.

## Minimum content pack to collect

- One agreed explanation of the guild's audience and relationship to AI building.
- A completed first-timer FAQ, including whether someone can attend alone, without existing members, or without a project.
- The actual endorsement/review process and what membership entails.
- A few permissioned photographs from real gatherings, with dates and useful captions.
- Two or three member project stories if available: the problem, contributors, what they did, result and link. If no projects are ready to publish, start with event recaps.
- Approved organiser/host introductions and a monitored contact.
- Current status and next step for every advertised programme.
- A truthful answer for a student who cannot travel to Metro Manila.
- A small set of real volunteer/partner requests, with an owner for each.

The suggested quantities are starter editorial targets, not research-derived minimums. Do not create invented stories, testimonials, partners, outcomes or participation numbers to fill a layout.

## Supporting guidance

[W3C's forms tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/) recommends short forms that collect only the information needed, with labels, instructions and clear success/error feedback. For the guild, use the existing RSVP service where appropriate; keep an enquiry form limited to information needed to respond. Avoid making unrelated profile information mandatory.

[Stanford's web credibility guidelines](https://credibility.stanford.edu/guidelines/index.html) support verifiable evidence, visible people, contact information and maintained content. This is foundational research published in 2002, not a current experiment establishing a conversion uplift for Seekers Guild.

[DEVCON's published privacy/safe-space page](https://devcon.ph/playbook/privacy-and-safe-space/) is an example of exposing these expectations and a contact. This is evidence of website content, not a legal template or an endorsement of every consent practice it describes. Any guild policy must reflect its own operations.

## Pseudocode Example

```text
visitor arrives
  -> understands audience and activities
  -> sees next public gathering and real community evidence
  -> chooses a route
       attend -> current event -> authoritative RSVP -> event confirmation
       membership -> eligibility and expectations -> endorsement/review instructions
       contribute -> actual role -> monitored contact
       partner -> available formats -> enquiry

editor prepares content
  -> verify facts with the responsible organiser
  -> confirm publication permission where relevant
  -> publish with date and a working next action
  -> review on programme, host or event changes
  -> expire or archive time-sensitive items
```

## Critical Quirks

- Public event attendance and formal membership are separate concepts in the current material. Use distinct labels and explain the relationship.
- Do not silently change the two-endorsement policy. Clarify how it operates and obtain guild decisions about missing mechanics.
- A regional mission does not establish active chapters, remote sessions, grants or travel support. Publish supported routes and identify ambitions as future plans.
- Keep Luma as the authoritative registration destination while it serves that need. A second RSVP system creates another place for status to drift.
- Date-sensitive content needs an owner, expiry/review date and useful empty state. The earlier code audit identified a risk that a saved future event could remain upcoming after its date; fix classification before expanding feeds.
- Public project or member stories require permission and accurate attribution. A public event attendee list is not permission to create member profiles.
- Sponsorship logos need accurate relationship labels. A former event co-host is not automatically an ongoing guild sponsor.
- Programme status should distinguish active, accepting participants, selective, completed and planned where those distinctions apply.
- Early-stage communities can demonstrate depth through a few specific stories. Large self-reported numbers on comparison sites do not justify invented guild metrics.
- This study is a desk review of public material and project source, not participant interviews. Validate the most important unanswered newcomer questions with actual first-time attendees before committing to a larger platform.

## Maintenance and validation

| Content | Suggested owner | Review trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Next event and logistics | Event host; Luma for registration | When an event is announced, changed, cancelled or completed |
| Programme details | Programme lead | When status, eligibility or next intake changes |
| Joining instructions | Membership lead | When the process changes or newcomers report confusion |
| Stories and photos | Content editor plus contributors | Before publication and on correction/removal requests |
| Roles and opportunities | Person receiving enquiries | When filled, expired or withdrawn |
| Team and contact details | Community lead | Whenever responsibility changes |

Validate comprehension with a few new visitors: can they identify whom the guild serves, find the next gathering, explain the attendance/membership distinction, and find a route without Manila travel? Use this as qualitative feedback, not a statistically representative study. Where measurement is available, distinguish attendance CTA clicks, completed RSVPs, first-time attendance and membership enquiries rather than calling every click a new member.

## When to Use

Use this plan when deciding what factual content to collect, which pathways the existing website should support, and what deserves prominence in its next iteration. Start with concrete information the guild can keep accurate, then add content depth as real activity accumulates.

## When Not to Use

Do not treat the comparison as proof that the guild should copy a larger organisation's account system, admission model, rewards, job marketplace or governance. A chatbot, gamified ranks, complex member portal, broad job board or empty blog is not established as a priority by this research. Revisit those features only when a real recurring task, active users and an operational owner justify them.

## Research limitations

The Superteam Philippines homepage could not be retrieved, so only its official public opportunity profile and linked first-party listing material informed that comparison. Some search results for Seekers Guild show an older indexed homepage; current deployment observations from the earlier direct fetch and browser inspection take precedence. Statistics on comparison sites are self-reported and were not independently audited. The recommendation does not assume those organisations' scale, funding or programmes are available to Seekers Guild.
