# Visual Theme Direction

## Rationale

The site follows the Seekers Guild visual identity from the guild's brand document: Sapphire, Heliodor, Moonshine and Obsidian; Cinzel, Lora and Montserrat; and "bold, gold typography against the dark blue background". Its one object is the compass from the Sigil of Seeking: "This compass does not point north. It points to the thing you want most in this world." The page is built from flat brand-colour bands that meet on angled cuts. Gold is flat Heliodor, with gold-framed panels, a low-poly compass (every curve cut into flat facets) inside a many-sided bezel engraved with the guild's taglines, and two public-domain impressionist paintings laid into the bands as atmosphere: a deep-blue one behind the dark bands and a light one behind the light and blue bands.

## Pseudocode Example

```text
Opening   Sapphire deep  "We seek those who seek" in Heliodor Cinzel + the compass, sized to the screen (needle follows the visitor)
Who       Moonshine      the Sigil of Seeking + who the guild is
Vision    Sapphire       the vision statement in Lora
Mission   Moonshine      three promises
Pillars   Obsidian       five pillars
Culture   Heliodor       ten values set large
Close     Sapphire deep  "Onward, fellow seeker"
bands meet on hard diagonal edges (.band--cut / .band--cut-left)
```

## Critical Quirks

- Band edges: the angled clip polygons run a few pixels past the sides and bottom, so no edge is anti-aliased exactly on the screen edge (which showed the page background as a hairline at fractional pixel ratios).
- Colours are the brand hexes, as tokens in `app/globals.css`. Flat colour only: no gradients, glows, drop shadows or new hues. Panels get their edge from the gold frame, not from shadows or offset plates.
- The compass is drawn low-poly: arcs and rings are runs of flat triangles in a few close tones of one colour, star points are four flat-shaded facets, the hub is an octagon. Each facet is stroked in its own fill so no seams show. Around it sits an instrument in three layers (`MagicCircle.tsx`): the low-poly bezel with the taglines and degree scale, a faint scanner ring of thin arcs and small nodes, and a still compass card with N/E/S/W and faint degree numerals every 30°. The technical touches stay low-key: thin, low-contrast, never louder than the compass. The cardinal letters never rotate. No six-pointed stars or crossed triangles anywhere.
- Compass colour: the official logo leads the palette: white/soft ivory rose points, subtle blue facet shadows, and bright Heliodor arcs and inner ring. Diagonal points use quieter ivory and blue tones. These colours are scoped to `.emblem` as `--compass-*` tokens. The navy bezel and gold-and-sapphire needle preserve the site's identity; geometry and flat shading stay the same. Page typography and actions continue using brand Heliodor.
- Cinzel Bold is the guild voice (headings, titles, display, all at 700); Lora carries long statements and quotes; Montserrat is for everything read quickly. All three are self-hosted via `next/font/local`.
- All copy comes from the guild's documents (`app/content/site.ts`). Founded April 2025.
- Ornaments live in `app/components/Ornament.tsx` (`Flourish`, `Ornate`) and `MagicCircle.tsx` (circle of light, motes). Painting layers are the `Paint` element in `app/page.tsx` with `.paint--*` classes; each sits under a veil so text keeps its contrast.
- Readability rule: Cinzel only for headings and short titles. Paragraphs are at least 17px, in near-full colour, never grey on grey. Panel titles use Lora.
- Buttons are square: a Heliodor gold fill, or a gold 1.5px line.
- Two layouts. Desktop (wider than 860px, which includes landscape tablets) is the composed page above. Phones and portrait tablets get their own touch design, all in the "Compact layout" section at the end of `app/globals.css`: the opening and the send-off are centred, each with the compass on the horizon beneath it (zoomed past the screen width, hub just above the section's foot, so only its upper half rises into view), a one-line intro and full-width buttons on phones; mission, pillars and programmes become swipe rows (one card per view on phones, two on tablets, the next peeking in) with diamond dots (`Rail.tsx`); the culture is one gold-framed list of the guild's values divided by fine gold rules (two columns on wide screens and tablets, one on phones); past gatherings open on the latest four with a "Show all" button (`PastEvents.tsx`); and a slim bar at the bottom shows the next gathering from Luma with a Reserve button (`JoinDock.tsx`), appearing once the opening's buttons scroll away and stepping aside for the send-off and footer. Desktop never renders any of it.
- Motion: the compass is the signature opening. Its dial arrives first, gold arcs and inner ring align from opposing bearings, then the white rose seats and the needle finds its bearing. Alignment takes 1.6 seconds with short offsets; the needle settles over 2.4 seconds after a 240ms offset. Its CSS linear easing samples the mass 1, stiffness 100, damping 10 spring at 81 points, stretched for a slower magnetic pull. Pointer interaction can interrupt at the visible angle. Supporting motion is limited to interaction feedback: a 140ms button press, 160ms navigation and FAQ indicators, a 220ms menu opening and 160ms closing fade, and a 200ms menu icon change. Keyboard actions remain immediate. Text, cards, ornaments, paintings and particles stay still; there are no scroll reveals. Wheel momentum and native card navigation are described below. Reduced motion keeps compasses and controls static.
- The needle responds only inside the round compass face. A damped angular spring (mass 1, stiffness 100, damping 10) pulls it along the shortest path, overshoots gently, then settles at the last direction. A small dead zone around the hub prevents jitter. Retargeting preserves momentum and interrupts the entrance at its current angle. The animation writes the needle's transform directly and stops scheduling frames at rest; scrolling, reduced motion, hidden tabs and window blur stop tracking motion.
- Both opening and closing compasses use `MagneticCompass.tsx`, with independent spring state and section-scoped pointer listeners. Their resting angles are 32° and 78° respectively. Each `[data-compass-face]` wrapper supplies the circular hit area; hovering either compass never steers the other.
- Input follows capabilities rather than screen width. An available fine pointer with hover enables mouse/trackpad tracking, including on tablets. Touch and pen attract only after release within 500ms and 10 CSS pixels of the starting point. Swipes, scrolling, cancelled gestures, multiple touches, long presses and touches on links or controls are ignored. All listeners are passive; native scrolling and pinch zoom remain available.
- Performance: no general animation library or animation assets. Lenis is isolated to the optional wheel-scrolling chunk. The reveal component, its observer, event queue, animation listeners, per-item styles and markup hooks are removed. Compass parts use independent HTML layers containing static SVGs. The compass opening and control transitions run in CSS without per-frame React updates. The compass bezel and scanner rotate slowly on all devices, including phones and tablets. Their CSS timelines run continuously while scrolling, reversing direction, opening menus and travelling off screen, including in data-saving mode. No scroll listener, settle timer or intersection observer pauses the rings. The bezel and scanner rotate HTML wrappers around small shared WebP textures of the authored ring vectors, with transform promotion limited to those four continuously moving layers. Responsive 600/1200/1800px variants preserve detail without live SVG work per frame. The fixed cardinal card remains SVG. Browser-managed content-visibility skips offscreen drawing while animation clocks keep advancing; it does not pause or restart them. Regenerate the textures with `node scripts/bake-compass-rings.cjs` after editing `scripts/assets/compass-bezel.svg`, `scripts/assets/compass-scan.svg` or their source-vector styles in globals.css. The baker uses the self-hosted Cinzel font, Chrome/Edge and the existing Sharp dependency; no browser code or extra runtime dependency is added. Ambient.tsx pauses them only in a hidden document; reduced motion disables them in CSS. The initial loading cover still hands off to the hero opening before its rings become visible. The magnetic needle releases its compositor hint when its spring stops. The header only updates state when crossing its background threshold. Paintings are static section backgrounds; pre-blended blue and royal files avoid live blending.

## Background texture

Small, irregular origami folds echo paper folded several times without replacing the paintings. `public/images/origami-folds.svg` is a shared, seamless static vector behind content bands only, at 8% layer opacity (6% on Moonshine). Uneven crease spacing, varied facet tones and section-specific offsets avoid a regular grid or repeated starting point. The tile renders at 960 by 800px, or 720 by 600px on compact screens, so tall sections keep the same fine fold scale. Its planes and paired highlight/shadow crease lines use only existing navy and white tones. Crease edges carry more contrast than the broad facets so folds remain visible over the paintings. The navbar has an opaque navy backing so the texture cannot show through; the footer has no texture layer. The texture has no animation, runtime randomness, live blending, filters, pointer handling or promoted compositor layer.

## Scrolling

`ScrollMotion.tsx` lazy-loads Lenis 1.3.27 only for a fine pointer with hover, after the initial loading cover clears. Wheel notches receive short exponential deceleration (lerp 0.18, distance multiplier 1). Small pixel gestures, touch, pinch zoom, horizontal card-rail gestures and menu content remain native. Vertical wheel input stays continuous while passing over card rails. The browser keeps its own touch momentum and supported edge bounce. No transformed page wrapper, section snapping or scroll reveals are added. Animation frames run only during active wheel momentum and stop at rest. Keyboard navigation, focus changes, pointer input, hash links and history interrupt it immediately. Reduced motion, data saving, hidden tabs and an open menu disable and tear down the controller. Rail dots use native smooth scrolling, or immediate movement for reduced motion. Exact edge elasticity follows the device and browser rather than a simulated whole-page bounce.

## Loading

The loading screen shows only a large faceted compass on deep navy, with no visible text. It seats into place as its gold arcs, inner ring and tick dial turn at opposing speeds. The needle seeks three bearings per four-second revolution, using the sampled magnetic spring between stops; its final angle matches the next loop without a snap. All moving parts are independent HTML layers with static SVGs, animated only with transform and opacity. The accessible status remains "Loading Seekers Guild". `LoadingScreen.tsx` is server-rendered and shared by the initial visit and `app/loading.tsx`; it requests no extra images or animation library. `BootLoader.tsx` clears the initial cover when hydration is ready, with a 220ms exit fade and no minimum display time. The hero's opening waits behind it. Keyboard use dismisses the cover immediately, a four-second fallback prevents failed hydration from trapping visitors, and no JavaScript leaves the initial cover hidden. Reduced motion uses a static mark and immediate exit.

## When to Use

Every page. A new section is a new band in one of the five brand colours, alternating light and dark.

## When Not to Use

Do not add monospace labels, numbered markers, fake metadata, glows or unrelated texture overlays. Background texture is limited to the faint folded-paper treatment described above.
